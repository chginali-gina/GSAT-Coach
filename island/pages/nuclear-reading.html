開始使用
給老師的小提醒：本教材依一般知識整理，並非取自任何影片內容。數據皆為約略值，上課前建議再核對最新資料，尤其是台灣核能政策的最新現況。

How It Works

如何進行：45 分鐘小組辯論

全班分成 6 組，每組扮演一種立場，說服全班。重點不是贏，而是學會用證據做取捨。

The Situation

情境：「陽光島」是一座人口密集的小島，約九成五的能源靠進口。2040 年前，島上必須大幅減少發電產生的碳排放。要蓋新的核能電廠？全力發展太陽能與風電？還是兩者並用？

課前準備

  • 把全班分成 6 組，每組 4–6 人。人數較少時，只用其中 4–5 張角色卡也可以。
  • 讓學生看到角色卡（投影，或請學生用手機開啟「角色卡」分頁）。

四個步驟

1

站隊暖身

5 MIN

老師唸：「核能是阻止氣候變遷的必要條件。」同意的站左邊，不同意的站右邊，不確定的站中間。每個人記下自己站在哪裡。

2

小組準備

10 MIN

每組抽一張角色卡，翻面讀完。一起準備 1 分鐘的發言：一個立場、兩個理由。組內分工：發言人、記錄員、提問人、計時員。

3

發言與提問

20 MIN

每組發言 1 分鐘。接著每組向另一組問一個問題（可以用角色卡上的「hard question」），被問的組要回答。老師指定誰問誰，讓立場相近的組也互相挑戰。

4

再站隊與反思

10 MIN

再站一次隊，看看誰移動了。每個人寫一小段話：我有沒有改變想法？是哪個證據或哪句話讓我改變（或沒有改變）？

怎樣算表現好？

有證據

至少說出一個數字或例子，不只是「我覺得」。

有回應

先聽懂對方，再回答，例如：“I understand your point, but …”

有禮貌

對事不對人。可以不同意，但要說明原因。

結束時可以問學生

  • 你改變想法，是因為證據，還是因為某個人講得比較好聽？
  • 如果你真的是陽光島的市長，你還想知道什麼資訊才能做決定？

Do We Need Nuclear Power to Stop Climate Change?

Climate change is mainly caused by burning such as coal, oil and gas. To slow it down, the world must cut its quickly. But how? Many scientists agree that we need to produce electricity without releasing much carbon dioxide. Nuclear power is one option. Wind and solar power are others.

Supporters of nuclear power point out that a produces electricity day and night, in any weather. Wind and solar are cheap and growing fast, but they are : the sun sets and the wind sometimes stops. Batteries help, but storing enough energy for days or weeks is still difficult. Nuclear plants also take up little land and, over their whole , release about as little carbon as wind farms. Statistics show that, per unit of energy, nuclear power has caused far fewer deaths than coal.

Critics answer that nuclear plants are expensive and slow to build. Several recent projects in Europe and the United States were years late and billions of dollars . Time matters, because climate have deadlines. Money spent on one big reactor might build much more wind and solar power, much faster. There is also the problem of , which must be stored safely for thousands of years, and the fear that follows accidents such as Fukushima in 2011.

So do we need nuclear power? Most climate models say it is helpful but not . Countries with lots of sun, wind or hydropower may not need it. Countries with little space or weak resources may find it more attractive. In the end, the question is not only about science. It is also about money, risk, and what kind of future people are willing to accept.

Key Takeaways

  • Most models say nuclear power helps, but the must-do is cutting fossil fuels fast.
  • Nuclear gives steady power; wind and solar are cheaper and quicker to build.
  • Nuclear waste and high costs are the biggest concerns; safety fears are strong, even though its death rate per unit of energy is low.
  • The core question is a trade-off: speed and cost, or keeping every low-carbon option open?

Discussion Questions

  1. Trade-offYou have $50 billion and 10 years. Would you build nuclear reactors, or solar, wind and batteries? What evidence would change your mind?
  2. Risk perceptionPeople fear nuclear accidents more than coal pollution, even though coal pollution kills far more people. Why do we judge some risks differently? Is that fear irrational?
  3. EthicsNuclear waste stays dangerous for thousands of years. Do we have the right to leave it to future generations, when climate change is also a burden we leave them?
  4. AssumptionsThe world may need much more electricity as cars, heating and AI data centers switch to it. How does that change the question "Do we need nuclear power?"
  5. Compare casesGermany closed its reactors, while France built many. Which choice would you defend? What would you need to know first?
  6. Local contextTaiwan imports almost all of its energy and has phased out its reactors, while the debate about restarting them continues. How should a small island weigh energy security, safety and emissions?
  7. ValuesSome environmental groups oppose nuclear power and others now support it. Why might people who share a goal disagree on the method?
  8. Decision-makingWho should decide: scientists, politicians or voters? What does each group get right, and what might each get wrong?
